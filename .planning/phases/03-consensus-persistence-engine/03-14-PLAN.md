  <behavior>
    - kill_restart_keeps_committed_writes: a 3-node FakeIo-backed cluster; write 100 keys (each acknowledged write is committed by quorum). Crash 1 (follower): a background task keeps issuing writes through the leader and records each acknowledged pair in the expected map; crash_with_unsynced_tail(follower, 7) sets the follower's sync delay, waits until its FakeIo reports more than 7 unsynced bytes, then crashes it; stop the background task; restart the follower: restart succeeds, recovery_stats().torn_bytes_discarded is greater than 0 (a record really was torn and was discarded rather than rejected), the node rejoins and converges. Crash 2 (leader): same procedure with keep 301 against the then-current leader while the background task writes through the surviving leader once one is elected; after restart its recovery_stats() is read and recorded in the failure message, and the sum of torn_bytes_discarded over both restarts is greater than 0. Final check: every acknowledged write is present on all three nodes and the three engines are identical (a write that was in flight at a crash and never acknowledged may be present or absent, but must be the same on every node)
    - the crashed node's restart never fails with a corruption error for a torn tail in its last segment
  </behavior>
  <action>
Implement crash, crash_with_unsynced_tail, fake_io and restart in the harness if not already done in Task 1: crash(id, keep) calls FakeIo::crash(keep) first (so the still-running writer thread can no longer persist anything, like a killed process), then shuts the node down, leaving the FakeIo state (durable image plus torn prefix); restart revives the FakeIo, clears its sync delay and starts the node from the same state. The previous version of this test could pass vacuously because without delayed fsync nothing is ever unsynced at crash time; the delay-then-wait-for-unsynced-bytes step is what guarantees a torn record exists, and the torn_bytes_discarded assertion is what proves the recovery path actually ran. Choose keep values that cut a record in half (7 and 301 bytes of unsynced data) in the two separate crashes.
